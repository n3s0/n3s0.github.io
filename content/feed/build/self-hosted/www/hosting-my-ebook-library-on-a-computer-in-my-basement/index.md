## Summary
---

This is a running document of how I'm self-hosting my eBook library in my
basement. I will discuss design, setup, pitfalls, and ideas for improvement.
Before I settled for the Amazon Kindle library. But, let's face it. When using
the Kindle library you don't actaully own the book. You're just given a copy of
the book in a proprietary format. Which after some time thinking about it. It
didn't sit well with me.

So, I embarked on a personal journey to understand a few aspects of Digital
Rights Management (DRM). Finding a few options from digital book resellers that
provide DRM free options and I'm moving forward with hosting my own eBook
library on a workstation. Mostly due to the cost of hardware and storage lately.
There will definately be improvements to infrastructure and the like as a result
of this. One of those things includes hosting a local [DNS](#) server on the
network.

I need an application that can help catalog a large variety of physical and
digital books, pull down metadata about the books, and maybe even track reading
progress while syncing to social media platforms like Goodreads or Hardcover.

If you would like to just skip to what I'm using and how to set it up. You can
do that [here](#).

I performed a little backup of most of my Kindle books and they are now hosted
on my own personal server that I control access to. A digital book should be no
different than a physical book in my opinion. I will explore a few posts on how
to get around the digital rights management on the books within Kindle libraries
in other posts (strictly for educational purposes).

## Why do this?
---

I have a tendacy of just buying books for the purpose of having them. I find a
lot of subjects interesting. I'm just not always in the mood to read about it
when I buy it. Think it's called being a bibliophile. But, who knows. 

With that comes having a large quantity of digital books that I'd rather host
myself. I paid the money for the book. Sometimes I even buy the physical copy
and use the digital version in the even that I forget it at home or something.

The whole point of this is to just have it for my own personal use in a format
that I can backup and not have to rely on other services and their engineers to
maintain.
